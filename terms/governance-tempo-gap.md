# Governance Tempo Gap

> The distance between the rate at which an agentic system changes its own state and the rate at which its Steward can perceive that state and act on it — a gap that widens as execution tempo rises, because the agent's side of Bandwidth Inversion moves in release cycles while the Steward's moves in generations, and whose size at any moment sets how much of the system remains governable without rerouting.

## Extended Definition

The market-computation arc made every participant in a market fast except the Steward, and The Slowest Interface in the Market named that as the arc's open gap without measuring it. The Governance Tempo Gap is the measurement. On one side is the rate at which the system changes its own state: commitments made, allocations recomputed, escalations raised, actions taken in the physical world. On the other is the rate at which the Steward can take in that state and put a decision back — the perception-and-response cycle on the verification and command routes. The gap is the distance between the two rates. It is not a property of any single interface; it is a property of the pair, at a moment in time.

What makes the gap a term rather than an observation is that it moves in one direction. Bandwidth Inversion is asymmetric in rate: the agent's efficiency profile improves in release cycles, the Steward's changes in generations. Every model release that makes agents faster at changing state widens the gap if the Steward's routes stay as they were. That is the same structural shape as Operational Arbitrage, where the difference between a human-heavy incumbent's cost and an autonomous business's cost widens as the technology improves. The Governance Tempo Gap is the arbitrage's uncomfortable twin: the same improvement that widens the margin also widens the distance between the system and the human responsible for it.

The gap's size at any moment determines how much of the system is governable. Below some ratio the Steward sees the system as it is and governs it. Above it, the Steward is governing a picture that lags the system, which is Verification Blindness, and past that the Steward disengages and Nominal MTTI records the disengagement as autonomy. Market Computation and the Executable Economy sit at the high end: an allocation rule that recomputes on every relevant change, across every participant, moves the state faster than any human reads a record of it. The Stewardship Model does not fail at that tempo. It fails if the Steward's routes were designed for a lower one and never revisited.

The gap is closed from one side only. The Steward cannot be made to perceive faster than a human perceives, so the correction is to change what the Steward has to perceive: route state to vision, compress it to what the governance task requires, and route intent through speech, so that the Steward's fixed rate is spent on the highest-value part of the cycle. That is the Transcoding Layer's job stated as a trend rather than a snapshot. Arco projects the gap as the variable the Stewardship Model must track as ventures move toward computed markets; it is a structural forecast, not a figure Arco has yet measured in operation.

### Application

Measure the gap as a ratio, not a feeling: state changes per Steward perception cycle on the verification route. When the ratio rises with each model release while the surface stays the same, the surface is not keeping up and the Transcoding Layer, not the Steward, is where the correction belongs.
