# License Grade

> The versioned progression of a Task License from junior to senior standing, based on an agent's accumulated, verified track record within its certified task domain — the same graduated trust a human professional earns through demonstrated performance over time, rather than a single pass-fail credential issued once and never revisited.

## Extended Definition

A Task License certifies competence at a single point in time — an agent is verified capable of a bounded task, or it is not. That static certification cannot capture something a human licensing regime has always accounted for: an agent that has resolved a thousand cases correctly within its certified domain has earned different standing than one certified yesterday with no track record at all. License Grade is the mechanism that captures the difference.

The grade is not a new certification event. It is the same Task License, reviewed periodically against the same evidentiary record that justified it initially — continued strength in Recovery Latency, continued low Rollback Cost, and a growing volume of correctly resolved cases within the certified domain — rather than assumed to hold indefinitely from a single initial verification.

Grade has direct architectural consequences, not merely ceremonial ones. A junior-grade license carries a narrower Intervention Threshold, escalating more edge cases to the Steward by design, the same caution applied to a newly hired specialist regardless of credentials on paper. A senior-grade license, earned through accumulated verified performance, can carry a wider threshold and should be priced differently under License Indemnity — a carrier pricing coverage against a senior grade, backed by real performance history, is pricing a materially different risk than the same nominal credential held by an agent with no track record yet.

A credible License Grade system must allow downgrades as readily as upgrades. An agent whose performance degrades within its certified domain should see its grade reviewed downward; a one-directional system that only ever promotes would eventually certify agents at a level of trust their current performance no longer supports.
