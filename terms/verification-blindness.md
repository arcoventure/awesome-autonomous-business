# Verification Blindness

> The condition in which a Steward on the verification route cannot perceive the system's current state at the tempo the system changes it — because state reaches the Steward through a channel that is not their fastest, so their picture of the system lags or stops while the system continues; the precursor to disengagement and to a Nominal MTTI reading.

## Extended Definition

Verification Blindness names a state, not a metric. Nominal MTTI is the reading an autonomy score gives when the Steward has stopped intervening because they have stopped looking; Verification Blindness is the condition that precedes it. The Steward is present, engaged, and looking at the surface they were given — and the surface delivers the system's state through a channel that is not the Steward's fastest, so the picture they hold falls behind the system that produced it. At low tempo the lag is tolerable and reads as a slow dashboard. At the tempo of a system that changes state faster than a human can read a record of the change, the lag becomes a gap, and the Steward is governing a picture of the past.

The condition follows directly from Bandwidth Inversion. The verification route carries state from agents to a human: the sender's native medium is structured text, the receiver's fastest input is vision. A route that delivers the sender's medium — the decision trail, the ledger as written, a log the Steward is expected to follow — hands the receiver their slowest channel for the one route where perceiving is the entire function. An audio-only route does the same thing from the other side: a spoken account of state cannot be scanned, cannot be read twice, and arrives in sequence when the Steward needs it in parallel. In both cases the Steward is not slow. The Steward is blind, and blind for a mechanical reason the Transcoding Layer exists to remove.

The distinction from adjacent terms is where the term earns its place. The Audit Surface is the artefact — the governance digest derived from the Proof of Action trail for the Steward's comprehension. Verification Blindness is what happens when that artefact, or its absence, fails to keep pace. Steward Experience is the discipline that holds the surface to a standard; Verification Blindness is the specific failure the standard exists to prevent. Nominal MTTI is the downstream lie the metric tells once the blind Steward has disengaged. Naming the middle term matters because the two ends already had names and the mechanism between them did not: a Steward who is blind first becomes a Steward who stops looking, and only then does the score improve.

At the Physical Intervention Threshold the condition is a safety matter rather than a governance one. The escalations that threshold produces are the ones where a human must see current state and answer before an irreversible action proceeds. A Steward in Verification Blindness at that gate is answering from a picture that is already wrong.

Arco treats Verification Blindness as a design outcome, not a competence failure. It is produced by a route, and it is removed by rerouting.

### Application

Test the verification route at the system's actual tempo, not at demo tempo: if the Steward cannot answer 'what did it just do' from the surface faster than the system does the next thing, the route is producing Verification Blindness regardless of how complete the surface is.
