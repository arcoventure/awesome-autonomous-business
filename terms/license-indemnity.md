# License Indemnity

> The narrow, per-task-class professional indemnity coverage a Task License unlocks — priced against the specific, certified competence of one agent for one bounded task domain, and structured to sit beneath a business's broader AI liability policy rather than compete with it.

## Extended Definition

Insuring the Autonomous Business established the system-level AI liability market forming around certifications like AIUC-1 — enterprise-first, priced against a business's overall agentic architecture. License Indemnity is a narrower layer beneath that market, priced against one specific, certified competence rather than an entire stack's aggregate risk.

The layering logic mirrors exactly how a licensed human professional's coverage already works. An individual's malpractice coverage is priced against their specific credential and the class of work it certifies, sitting beneath their firm's broader professional liability policy — the firm absorbs residual and aggregate exposure, the individual's license-tied coverage absorbs the specific, scoped risk a credentialed professional was independently verified competent to carry. A Task License is what makes an agent's equivalent of that individual credential possible; License Indemnity is the coverage the credential unlocks.

This produces materially more precise pricing than a system-level policy alone, because a carrier underwriting License Indemnity is pricing a specific, verified competence rather than the unpredictability of an entire stack. As a Task License progresses through License Grade — junior to senior, based on accumulated Recovery Latency and Rollback Cost history — the premium a carrier assigns should track that earned standing, the same way a human professional's premium reflects a growing clean track record rather than remaining fixed at first-year rates indefinitely. This mechanism is explicitly a market direction this body of work argues for, not a product available for purchase today; the underlying evidentiary discipline is worth building regardless of when the market matures enough to price it this precisely.

### Application

License Indemnity is priced against a Task License the same way a chartered accountant's individual malpractice coverage is priced against their specific credential and the specific class of work it certifies. The firm's broader professional liability policy — a business's system-level AI liability policy, in the autonomous business equivalent — absorbs residual and aggregate exposure; License Indemnity absorbs the specific, scoped risk a credentialed agent was independently verified competent to carry. As Task License progresses through License Grade from junior to senior standing, the premium should reflect the accumulated verified track record, the same way a human professional's malpractice premium reflects years of clean practice differently than a first year of licensure.

### Context

License Indemnity produces a more precisely priced risk pool than system-level coverage alone, because a carrier pricing it is underwriting a specific, certified competence for a specific, bounded task rather than the aggregate unpredictability of an entire agentic stack. A business with strong Task Licenses across its highest-exposure task classes should, in principle, see its broader AI liability premium reflect that — the same way a firm staffed entirely with licensed, credentialed professionals prices differently than one that is not. This mechanism depends on a market maturity that does not yet exist: carriers underwriting per-task-class coverage this precisely is an argument this body of work makes for the market's direction, not a description of a product currently available for purchase.
